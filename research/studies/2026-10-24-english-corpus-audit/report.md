# Building a trustworthy English keyboard corpus

**GECKO's current corpora are good enough to find strong English letter layouts, but not yet enough to claim an optimal general-English keyboard.** The main shortfall is source diversity and preserved typing information, rather than a lack of characters. Existing searches transfer well between novels and Shai under GECKO's score, so this is not a case of an optimizer producing only corpus-specific curiosities. However, several corpus names are transformations of the same ten books, the word lists describe an unusual workload, and the full-keyboard data has concrete preprocessing and simulation problems. My recommendation is to keep Shai as the provisional letter-layout baseline, repair the complete-text pipeline, and add a modest, source-separated English evaluation suite before running much more optimization. The intended target should be broadly useful written English; programming and Vim should later have separately measured workloads.

Audit date: 2026-10-24. This analysis used repository code, small compiled corpus files, existing evaluation artifacts, and primary-source documentation. It ran no builds, optimization, large downloads, or participant studies. Historical evaluation results were inspected, not reproduced. Detailed evidence and the lightweight counting script are in the [audit notes](corpus_audit.md), [generalization notes](overfitting.md), and [source notes](sources.md).

## The collection is useful, but narrower than it looks

The table separates counted symbol occurrences from independent evidence. Sizes are the compiled monogram totals; synthetic normalized counts are not observations. The corpus variants differ in tokenization, so their totals differ slightly even when their raw books are identical. ([Count audit](counts.json))

| Corpus | Audited size | What it contributes | Recommended role |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `english` | 7,485,869 symbols | Ten Gutenberg novels; historical replay baseline | Fiction validation and reproducibility |
| `english-cased` | 7,510,472 symbols | Same books, current tokenizer and Shift counts | Case-aware fiction validation |
| `english-full` | 7,528,900 symbols | Same books, full ASCII symbols and paragraph Enter | Full-text pipeline baseline, after repair |
| `english-full-bspc` | Synthetic mixture | Same books plus simulated errors and corrections | Set aside pending sampler repair and validation |
| `shai` | 520,397,321 symbols | Frequency-weighted imported word counts, documented as iWeb-derived | Provisional letter-layout training baseline |
| `reddit` | 55,633,851 symbols | Imported comment-style word counts | Informal-language validation, with provenance caveats |
| `mt-10k` | 81,669 symbols | Word list, each word once | Common-vocabulary stress test |
| `mt-450k` | 4,775,076 symbols | Much larger word list, each word once | Rare-vocabulary stress test |

**`english` and `english-cased` have exactly identical letter-only mono-, bi-, tri-, and skip-1 distributions.** They are valuable tokenizer variants, but success on both is not evidence of success on two independent English populations. The larger full-text and Backspace variants likewise add no new authors or genres. Ten long books give many repetitions of common patterns while retaining a concentrated era, genre, and author selection. Ulysses and Moby Dick alone account for about 36% of the local raw book-body characters; the build adds file counts without balancing books. ([Artifact and source audit](corpus_audit.md))

Shai is the most defensible existing starting point for the letter core. Its size and frequency weighting offer considerably broader lexical coverage than the novels or uniformly weighted vocabulary lists. Nevertheless, the published iWeb corpus is 14 billion words from 2017 web text; GECKO's approximately 520 million-symbol aggregate is a derivative, not that entire dataset. The exact cmini extraction, cleaning, sampling, and deduplication chain remains insufficiently documented. Its label does not establish how much email-like writing, news, documentation, advertising, or duplicated web text it contains. ([iWeb publisher](https://www.english-corpora.org/iweb/help/iweb_name.asp), [publisher catalog](https://www.english-corpora.org/corpora.asp?b=y), [cmini Shai directory](https://github.com/Apsu/cmini/tree/master/corpora/shai))

Reddit adds a genuinely different intended register, but unknown sampling dates, communities, authors, and bot filtering constrain interpretation. Its imported word counts also lose original sequence and case information. Neither “large” nor “conversational” guarantees representative everyday typing. Word lists have a different problem: equal weight per word makes a rare word count as often as a common one. MT-450k is useful for detecting pathological rare combinations; it should not receive the same authority as natural-frequency prose when choosing the primary English layout. ([Representation](../../../context/representation.md), [source audit](sources.md))

The measured distributions explain why several domains matter: after restricting to letters, Shai versus novels differs by **3.61% total variation for single letters, 9.45% for bigrams, and 19.30% for trigrams**. Shai versus Reddit has 15.55% trigram variation; Shai versus MT-450k reaches 34.42%. Total variation measures redistributed probability mass, not score loss or the fraction of keys that should move. Agreement about common letters therefore does not imply agreement about the sequences that drive layout scoring. ([Measured distribution comparison](counts.json))

## Existing transfer results are encouraging, with specific limits

The Phase 2 study compared matching symbol sets on two boards and found wins on the corpus that each search did not use. Calculated from its rounded holdout table, the score reduction against the best same-set reference is **6.38% for ANSI novels→Shai, 12.13% for ANSI Shai→novels, and 9.69%/9.87% in the two Corne directions**. These are substantial model-score margins. They support the conclusion that GECKO discovers useful letter arrangements across the tested distributions. They are not measured improvements in typing speed or comfort. ([Phase 2 evaluation](../../../context/evaluation.md))

The broader saved comparison gives Shai's winner first or second place in all five corpus cells on both boards, among six tested layouts. That strengthens its provisional status. But the average rank counts the novel family twice, includes the vocabulary stress distribution, and covers only that small candidate pool. It is not an estimate of performance across five independent everyday-English domains. The saved table also corrects a small prose error: on ANSI Reddit, Shai's winner ranks first and the novel-trained winner second; the latter is first on Corne Reddit. ([Saved cross-corpus table](../../../bench/phase2-2026-10-23/corpora.txt))

The novel-trained winner's weaker MT-450k result is evidence of distribution sensitivity, not proof that optimizing common prose is wrong. Rare vocabulary should remain rare in a natural-frequency target. The decisive question is whether a candidate loses materially on another important writing domain, such as messages or explanatory prose. Equally, agreement among 30 search seeds establishes optimizer consistency under fixed inputs; it does not measure uncertainty about the English distribution. The existing experiment also pinned Corne thumbs and searched the same 30 symbols as its references, so it does not validate full freedom over Space, modifiers, punctuation, and thumb placement. ([Evaluation setup and limits](../../../context/evaluation.md))

There is no unique corpus-independent optimum for “English.” An optimum requires a workload distribution, geometry, symbol coverage, and objective. A useful project target is therefore a layout with low modelled cost across a declared range of ordinary English writing, with no large loss in a major domain. Corpus robustness and scoring-model validity remain separate questions.

## Complete-keyboard optimization needs pipeline repairs first

For a letter layout, losing case or reconstructing some word boundaries can still leave a useful approximation. For a complete keyboard, those omissions directly determine whether Shift, punctuation, Space, and Enter seem worth accommodating. The word importer reconstructs word boundaries and estimates some cross-word sequences under independent-word assumptions; it cannot recover real sentence context or capitalization from lowercase word counts. Combining case-free Shai with a case-aware corpus does not turn absent Shift observations into measured low Shift use. ([Corpus representation](../../../context/representation.md), [implementation audit](corpus_audit.md))

The audit found two concrete problems. **Curly double quotes are not folded by the tokenizer.** The ten local novel files contain roughly 34,000 curly double quotes and no ASCII double quotes; those curly characters become unknown-character boundaries, including in `english-full`. Consequently, a corpus rich in dialogue substantially omits the corresponding quotation-key demand. This matters particularly for punctuation and Shift-pair placement. It should be repaired and tested with small explicit tokenizer fixtures before deriving a replacement corpus. ([Raw inventory](raw_inventory.json), [code audit](corpus_audit.md))

**The synthetic Backspace corpus cannot currently be trusted as “English plus corrections.”** Compared with `english-full`, Space falls from about 17.75% to 12.37%, while slash rises from about 0.00093% to 0.366%, roughly 394-fold. The inspected sampler draws a new random number inside each binary-search predicate comparison, rather than drawing one value and locating it in the cumulative distribution. This violates the predicate's monotonicity requirement. The generator also mixes a whole simulated stream into the corpus, so its higher-order and Shift statistics require validation even after that defect is fixed. The observed distortion and the code defect are established; a controlled repair-and-rebuild comparison was not run. Preserve the existing file for replay, but set it aside for new conclusions about a full English keyboard. ([Counts](counts.json), [sampler evidence](corpus_audit.md))

Coverage needs explicit control too. The scorer conditions on the symbols a layout contains and normalizes its tables afterward. A layout that omits difficult punctuation can therefore appear cheaper. Comparisons must require the same output repertoire and report excluded character and n-gram mass. Likewise, corpus mixing normalizes each table separately; its weights are a scoring mixture, not automatically the exact statistics of concatenated real typing. ([Scorer build](../../../crates/gecko-core/src/scorer/build.rs), [corpus mixing](../../../crates/gecko-core/src/corpus.rs), [keyset limitations](../../../context/keysets.md))

## Add source diversity before adding billions of characters

The next acquisition should be a small collection of raw sequences with document identity, capitalization, punctuation, and paragraph structure. Three useful high-level strata are ordinary written communication, explanatory/informational prose, and narrative prose. This is a practical definition of the intended target, not a measured claim about how all English users divide their typing. Keep historical fiction as a distinct check, and include American/British or international sensitivity rather than silently equating one variety with all English.

**MASC is a practical first screening addition:** about 500,000 words across 19 written and spoken genres, including email, letters, blogs, and informational text, with CC BY 3.0 licensing. Select relevant written slices rather than blindly giving spam, transcripts, and ordinary messages equal influence. Its smaller slices can reveal broad weaknesses but cannot certify tiny ranking differences. MASC is primarily drawn from OANC, so using all OANC for training and MASC for testing would risk overlap. ([MASC overview](https://anc.org/data/masc/), [genre composition](https://anc.org/data/masc/corpus/))

OANC offers roughly 15 million words with unrestricted use and redistribution according to its publisher, and can expand selected genres if the smaller screening exercise exposes a need. It is not a current-2026 sample: its material dates from 1990 onward and includes older web journalism. A version-pinned contemporary informational source can complement it. A small Wikipedia article sample preserves provenance but requires markup cleanup and does not represent messaging; Leipzig offers small dated sentence samples but shuffles sentences, so it cannot supply natural paragraph/Enter patterns. The selected Leipzig release's reuse terms still require checking. ([OANC](https://anc.org/data/oanc/), [OANC contents](https://anc.org/data/oanc/contents/), [Leipzig format](https://www.wortschatz.uni-leipzig.de/documents/Format_Download_File-eng.pdf), [Leipzig FAQ](https://wortschatz.uni-leipzig.de/en/documentation/faq), [Wikimedia reuse terms](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Terms_of_Use?useformat=mobile))

Do not combine sources in proportion to their downloadable size: the largest convenient archive would then decide what “English” means. Start with an explicitly declared mixture of high-level strata, compare it with Shai alone, and vary the weights over plausible alternatives. Equal strata is a transparent experimental baseline, not an empirically optimal weighting. The valuable output is evidence that the recommendation remains good across these choices.

## Judge sufficiency by stable decisions on fresh sources

Split raw data before aggregation by complete document and, where possible, author, site, thread, or book. Deduplicate across those groups. Randomly splitting characters or n-gram counts from the same texts measures sampling noise while sharing most source-specific structure; it cannot demonstrate transfer to new kinds of writing. Reserve fresh sources for a final test. The repeatedly inspected English/Shai/Reddit suite remains useful development evidence, but repeatedly choosing mixtures or winners against it makes it validation data. Model-selection criteria themselves can be overfit. ([Cawley and Talbot, 2010](https://www.jmlr.org/beta/papers/v11/cawley10a.html))

When CPU capacity returns, begin by rescoring a small frozen pool of saved winners and strong references, not by launching another large search. Report paired score differences and raw metrics by domain, with identical symbol coverage. Bootstrap documents or source groups to assess the uncertainty of those differences, preserving domains; this requires rescoring frozen layouts rather than optimizing every resample. Test a small set of mixture alternatives and final 100% trigram coverage, because the normal profile truncates to 99%. Then optimize only where those results justify it, freeze the finalists, and use the fresh final test once. These checks estimate corpus sensitivity under the chosen score, not human performance. ([Implementation and protocol notes](overfitting.md))

**The corpora are “enough” when more independent sources stop changing the practical recommendation**, plausible genre mixtures do not create unacceptable losses, and preprocessing or synthetic-action assumptions are smaller than the claimed advantage. Declare acceptable losses before the final test; no universal token count or validated numerical tolerance currently exists here. If several candidates remain indistinguishable, retain a robust near-optimal set instead of presenting a tiny aggregate score difference as a uniquely best keyboard.

The immediate priorities are therefore to preserve historical artifacts, repair and version the full-text/synthetic pipeline, document provenance, and design a genuinely independent small English suite. There is no need to discard the existing letter-layout results or acquire an enormous corpus first. A modest amount of better-chosen evidence can resolve more uncertainty than another order of magnitude of repeated web or novel text.
